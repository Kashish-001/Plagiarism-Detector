Renewable energy is turning into an important part of the worldwide electricity supply. Solar panels change sunlight directly into electrical power with photovoltaic cells. Wind turbines produce electricity by capturing the kinetic energy of moving air. Many countries have started subsidies to encourage households to install solar panels. The cost of producing solar electricity has dropped sharply during the last decade. But the output of solar and wind plants depends strongly on the weather. Large batteries can store extra electricity and release it when demand is high. Governments also invest in smart grids to balance supply and demand more efficiently. Critics say that the initial cost of installation stays too high for poorer communities. Despite these challenges, experts expect renewable sources to dominate new power capacity in the coming years.
